### Q2 {#INT-005.Q2}
Asked of: PER-RE · Task: P-2
**A2 {#INT-005.A2}** respondent: simulated · tag: SIMULATED · basis: [[INT-001.A1]]
> The risk executive function is the Component risk board, a group. The senior accountable
> official for risk management chairs it and set its structure in its charter; the CIO, the
> CISO, the privacy officer and the AOs sit on it. Its quarterly enterprise risk forum takes
> every type and source of risk, including aggregated risk across systems and shared
> platforms. It oversees risk decisions by reviewing every High and Very High decision and a
> sample of the others, and builds a broad view from the portfolio risk register. It shares risk
> through a quarterly risk posture report to AOs and senior leaders, the portfolio dashboard
> for program staff, and briefings to mission partners and the DoD CIO. Subordinate commands
> and PEOs may add their own assumptions to the frame, choose assessment methods within ours,
> respond to Moderate and lower risk on their own and set monitoring frequencies at or above
> ours. Where two AOs share an authorization, the board names a lead AO and the two sign a
> joint memorandum.

### Q3 {#INT-005.Q3}
Asked of: PER-RE · Task: P-2
**A3 {#INT-005.A3}** respondent: simulated · tag: SIMULATED · basis: [[INT-005.A1]]
> Risk assessments follow SP 800-30 Revision 1: a semi-quantitative five-level scale, threat
> and vulnerability based, run by the ISSM with the ISSE, reviewed by the SCA, at moderate rigor
> for business systems and high rigor for mission systems. Results go into a risk register with
> a risk statement, likelihood, impact and level. We use adversarial, accidental, structural
> and environmental threat sources from Component threat briefings and CSSP reporting, and
> threat events from SP 800-30 Appendix E. Vulnerabilities come from scans, assessments and
> POA&Ms; external dependencies such as cloud and platform providers are assessed as
> sources of risk. Impacts are on missions, assets, individuals, other organizations and the
> Nation over a one-year horizon. Likelihood combines initiation and impact likelihood;
> uncertainty is stated with each rating; risk is aggregated by shared platform and mission
> thread. Information sources: CSSP alerts, CISA and DoD advisories, scan data and SCA
> reports.

### Q4 {#INT-005.Q4}
Asked of: PER-RE · Task: P-2
**A4 {#INT-005.A4}** respondent: simulated · tag: SIMULATED · basis: [[INT-004.A2]]
> We use acceptance, avoidance, mitigation, sharing and transfer. The AO picks a response by
> comparing the risk with the tolerance and the cost of each course of action; acceptance is
> within the AO's limits only, avoidance is used for unacceptable connections or functions,
> mitigation through controls and POA&M items, sharing or transfer through providers and
> agreements. Courses of action are compared with a simple cost, effect and schedule table.
> Responses are recorded in the POA&M and the decision document and briefed to the board.

Asked of: PER-RE · Task: P-2
**A5 {#INT-005.A5}** respondent: simulated · tag: SIMULATED · basis: [[INT-005.A3]]
> Risk assessments are kept current: a system updates its assessment at least annually, and at
> once after a significant change, a new High or Very High finding, a relevant threat advisory

> vulnerabilities, likelihood, impact and the risk register. The ISSM sends the result to the AO
> and the board's portfolio register within 30 days.
