### F-12-1: No rule ties a partial or planned SSP status, an open STIG finding or a disclosed limitation to the SAR determination
- Where: A-3; `src/rmf_tool/check/rules/assessment.py` (ASMT-005 `determinations`), `rigor.py` (RIGR-002); `playbooks/A/A-3.md` step 4.2 last bullet; `personas/PER-SCA.md` role-play guidance
- Kind: gap
- Severity: major
- What happened:
  - The playbook and the SCA card both say that an SSP status of `partial` or `planned`, an open STIG finding mapped to the control, or a failed test makes the control `other-than-satisfied` unless evidence shows the gap closed.
  - ASMT-005 checks only that the statements are complete and consistent with the control result, that methods and reused results are in the plan, and that findings name what they affect. RIGR-002 checks depth and coverage.
  - Among the rules, only the POA&M rules read the status of a finding in `stig-summary.yaml` (`_finding_states`), and no rule reads `ssp/controls/<fam>.yaml` `status` against `sar/results/<fam>.yaml` `result`. So `ia-5: satisfied` while STG-002 (`control_id: ia-5`, `status: open`) is open would pass every rule. So would `au-11: satisfied` while the SSP says `partial`.
  - In this batch, all eight partial or planned controls (au-11, ca-2, ca-3, ca-5, ca-7, cm-6, cp-2, cp-4) and both STIG-mapped controls (cm-6, ia-5) were judged other-than-satisfied only because the agent followed the playbook. Nothing would have caught a softer assessor. This is the SAR side of F-10-7.
  - I found this by reading the rule source, not by an experiment that failed.
- Workaround used: Followed the playbook by hand. Every partial or planned SSP control and every control with an open STG item is other-than-satisfied with a finding.
- Recommendation: Add a rule, for example SAR-CONS-001, applying after A-3. When an SSP control is `partial` or `planned`, or has an open or risk-accepted STIG finding, a `satisfied` determination must carry a `gap_closed` note with evidence, or the rule fails. Charge it to A-3.
