### F-08-1: LA-01 as `external-service` passes every rule, so the run now gives one provider three different roles without complaint
- Where: `artifacts/data/leveraged-authorizations.yaml` LA-01; MLEV-001 (`monitor.py` 262-265); RECIP-001; CLOUD-001; SSPI-001; `allocation.yaml` pe-3, mp-6 (`common`); `org-context.yaml` common_controls (P-5); OCTX-004/005
- Kind: gap
- Severity: minor
- What happened: Under the ruling, LA-01 (Example GovCloud IL4 region) was re-recorded as `external-service`, with the package review that RECIP-001 then requires. pe-3 and mp-6 stay allocated as `common` to the same provider, and their statements stay `inherited`. On the first attempt, C-1, P-2, P-5, P-16, S-3, S-4 (cloud and SVCA rules) and a plain check all reported 0 errors. MLEV-001 checks one direction only: a `common-control-provider` record must supply a `common` allocation. It never checks that the provider of a `common` allocation holds a `common-control-provider` record, or any leveraged record. The run is now consistent enough for the rules, but one provider holds three roles: a common control provider in P-5's `common_controls` and in S-3's allocation, an `external-service` in C-1's leveraged record, and a `cloud-service-provider` in SVA-002. Nothing says whether that combination is intended. Downstream, the change makes MRPT-001 (M-5) require every change record to name `external_service`, and makes RECIP-001 require that LA-01's package review date precede the authorization decision. Both are correct for a cloud PA.
- Workaround used: As ruled. The leveraged-authorizations header comment, the journal NOTE (04:41Z), INT-117.A1 and ART-SVCA explain that the offering is an external service whose controls are inherited through its customer responsibility matrix.
- Recommendation: Decide the model and state it in the C-1 and S-4 playbooks and in the schema: "a cloud service offering is `external-service`, and its whole-inherited controls are still allocated `common`". Then either make MLEV-001 accept that pairing explicitly, or let `arrangement` take a list (F-06-2). Add the reverse check that the provider of every `common` allocation has a leveraged record (or an enterprise or P-5 package), so that a common allocation to an unauthorized provider fails.
